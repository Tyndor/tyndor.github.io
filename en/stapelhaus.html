Stapelhaus

Who lives where? Your building at a glance

Work in progress

Stapelhaus keeps track of who lives where in a building. You set up your building — name, floors, floor plan — and then record who lives in which apartment. For property managers, landlords, and anyone who wants to keep track of things in a larger building.

The building sits there as a stack of soft building blocks: the ground floor at the bottom, the upper floors above it, each floor in its own color. Tapping a floor opens its top-down view — the floor plan seen from above, with the stairwell, hallway, and elevator in their places. You're looking into the building from the front door: street at the bottom, courtyard at the top, so that left and right always mean the same thing.

Each apartment has the people who live there: last name, first name, phone number, and a note for anything that doesn't fit into a field. Whoever moves out doesn't vanish but moves into the apartment's history. Apartments with no residents are marked as either vacant or unknown — the difference between "nobody lives there" and "I don't know" is a big one in everyday life.

A list shows all the apartments in the building, with search across names, doorbell labels, and notes. The search also works in the floor plan: as you type, you see which apartment you mean.

What you enter stays on the device — no account, no sign-in. Backups are something you do yourself: you save them to a file and load them back from there. The free version shows ads; a one-time purchase removes them for good, and anyone who wants to manage more than one building can do so with a subscription. The two are independent of each other: if you only have one building, you don't need a subscription.
