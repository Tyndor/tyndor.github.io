Office Fauna

The satirical tracker for your meetings

Office Fauna measures how much of your working time goes into meetings and phone calls, and whether that time was well spent. One tap starts the timer, with no account and no setup. It keeps running when the app is closed: on Android as a notification with pause and end, on iPhones running iOS 16.1 or later as a Live Activity on the lock screen. If you like, set a planned duration first; the timer then shows live how far the meeting is running over. Forgotten conversations can be added afterwards and entries edited later. Each entry can have a title and a note, and while you type the app suggests titles from earlier entries.

Every conversation gets a rating on a five-step scale from sunny to thunderstorm, if you like while it is still running. The statistics show, by day, week, month and across all entries, how much time went into good, neutral and bad conversations, with a comparison to the previous period and a projection for the whole year. Overruns show up in the logbook and in the statistics too.

59 behavior patterns can be identified, disruptive and helpful ones alike, through three questions about observed behavior rather than a list of names: in what kind of conversation does the person show up, what do they do, what drives them? Each pattern comes with an explanation of why someone acts that way, concrete suggestions for handling it with example sentences you can copy, and a short self-test asking whether you might be showing that pattern yourself right now.

Everything you enter stays on your device: no account, no sign-in, no cloud. For backups and switching devices the app exports a file that you keep yourself, and an entry in the settings deletes all data on request. The free version shows ad banners through Google AdMob; this transfers advertising and technical data; details are in the app's privacy policy. Premium is a one-time purchase: no more ads, plus the "Meeting quality by species" analysis. Which behavior patterns statistically bring the bad weather?

The app works in portrait and landscape and on tablets too; in landscape and on large screens the navigation moves to the side and pages arrange themselves in columns. If you prefer less motion, you can reduce the animations, and large system fonts are respected instead of text being cut off. Office Fauna is available in English and in German, with editions for Germany, Austria and Switzerland and variants for Liechtenstein, Luxembourg, East Belgium and South Tyrol. The language follows your system setting or can be chosen in the settings, with a preview of how dates, numbers and quotation marks will look.

All of this is wrapped in a field guide. A conversation is an expedition, the rating a weather report, an identified behavior pattern a sighting, and the 59 patterns are species with Latin names, field notes, typical calls and their own illustration in the style of a Victorian natural history book. Identify a species for the first time and it unlocks in your collection. That is not an end in itself: nobody keeps a tally of bad meetings for long; a collector's album is another matter. What gets caricatured is behavior in conversations only, never people. The species texts were written with AI assistance and editorially reviewed.
